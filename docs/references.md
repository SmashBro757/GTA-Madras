# References

Two public efforts are noted here because they make a tradeoff concrete: production scale, a recognizable city, and realism. They pull against each other. A small fictional city faces that same tradeoff. These notes do not pick a point on it, and they do not treat either effort as a template, a partner, or an affiliation.

They are also not a claim that this idea is further along, or that Chennai is a better subject than Hyderabad or Bengaluru. What someone played, and what a page or a post merely says, are kept separate below.

A third public work, an existing Chennai game also called Project Madras, is recorded further down for a different reason: so the shared name is not confusing. It is not used as a comparison of scale, cost, or quality. "GTA-style" in the Bengaluru note means that author's genre description. In this repository the same phrase means genre and fictionalization only, not borrowed assets or branding.

## Hyd World

Public page: <https://gtahyd.in/>

A read of the public page on 7 November 2026 returned the title **Hyd World**, the place line **HITEC City · Hyderabad**, and the labels Download, Start, City, and Streets. These notes take that, and no more, from the page itself.

Venkat's firsthand account of playing it:

- It was freely and publicly playable, and it was multiplayer.
- Another player shot him.
- An area of Hyderabad where he had stayed was recognizable, and still rough.
- A bike passed with a player-chosen name, Revanth Reddy. The name was not scripted. It made him laugh.

That account is the context for the optional early opening in the vision notes. The laugh belongs to someone else's unscripted multiplayer session. It is not a brief to put political names into this project. The vision notes leave political set pieces out, including a convoy idea that was raised and then dropped.

## How to read the pair

In Venkat's account, Hyd World could already be entered, and a place he knew was already recognizable, while the result was still rough. Prajwal Shetty's post describes a heavier production aimed at a recognizable Bengaluru, including a dated flashback, at a stated early day count, with the picture itself unchecked. Together they are a reminder of the tradeoff. They are not a specification.

## An existing Project Madras game

Video Venkat watched: [Project Madras - OpenWorld Gameplay Showcase](https://youtu.be/ra-lYQAhbB8?si=ClY5YFNWclMkVFTL), on the YouTube channel [Project Madras](https://www.youtube.com/@ProjectMadras).

The title and the channel name are how the public page identifies the video. Venkat's clarification of 7 November 2026, 20:29 IST, is the rest of what these notes use. He has watched the video. He describes years of hard work. He loves their project. He describes the game as an existing independent commercial Chennai game, made by other people. He acknowledges that its makers may be motivated by love. These notes do not treat commercial work as the absence of care.

The video was not reviewed for scope, cost, or history, and those things are not stated from it. The word "open world" in the title is not a finding about the size of their city. Nothing here estimates their budget, their team, their sales, or their schedule, and nothing here ranks their game against this idea.

This idea is a fictionalized, much smaller, Chennai-inspired city, cut toward core highlights, and aimed at density of places, activities, and micro-interactions. It is not a competing full-city recreation, and it is not an imitation of their game. Their game is also not the sister repository below. The shared name is a collision, not an identity.

## Sister repository

[Project Madras](https://github.com/SmashBro757/Project-Madras) is distinguished in the [vision notes](vision.md). It is Venkat's separate idea: geographically grounded present-day Chennai, and a historical reconstruction with accurate change in time, terrain, and infrastructure. His clarification treats that repository, like this one, as an idea and a personal pet project, not as active development.

It is linked so this fictionalized idea does not inherit the sister brief, and does not inherit a one-to-one city, merely because the two repositories share an author and a city. These sentences are not that repository's own text.

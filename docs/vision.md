# Vision

A paused personal idea: a fictionalized city inspired by Chennai, small on purpose, and dense with the highlights of lived life there. The visual ambition is realistic. Cartoon stylization sits outside that ambition.

The city is personal to Venkat. These notes exist so that, if he ever picks the idea up, it has a chance of doing that lived city justice. A caricature would miss the point. So would a fictional city that pretends to be a record of the real one.

The setting is fiction. Fiction is the city in the idea, not a fabricated history of Chennai offered as fact. These notes claim no special rank for Chennai among cities. They do not invent a past for the real city, and they do not grade this idea against anyone else's game.

## Where the idea stands

Clarified 7 November 2026, 20:29 IST. Both of Venkat's repositories, this one and the [sister repository](https://github.com/SmashBro757/Project-Madras), are ideas and personal pet projects. Neither is active development.

Venkat is occupied for roughly the next month with leaving a job and with career matters. No restart date is set. These notes do not commit him, or anyone else, to resume.

If the idea ever continues, he intends it as personal and unpaid, and open to community contribution. Open source remains an intention. No license has been selected. That intention creates no team and authorizes no work.

## What the fiction has to be able to hold

A small city can still feel false if it keeps only one kind of Chennai. The highlights he cares about are:

- the IT corridor
- older industry
- the beaches
- temples and devotional life
- cultural history
- historic buildings that are still standing
- family life as it is lived at different ages

Read the list as the range a Chennai-inspired fiction ought to be able to suggest. Venkat's 80/20 names the cut: core highlights, not the whole geography. Treating the list as a map, a content schedule, or a promise would over-read it. No neighborhood has been chosen. No building has been designated. No activity and no micro-interaction has been specified. The desired quality is density, a small geography full of interesting places and things to do, and the detail of that density is undecided.

Historic buildings, here, are part of the lived feeling a fiction might echo. Rebuilding earlier coastlines, streets, and infrastructure as they changed through time is a different task, kept with the sister idea below.

## Scale

The geography in this idea is much smaller than the real city, and it is selective about which highlights it keeps. The limit is deliberate. Manageable is the goal. A one-to-one city, and a full-city recreation, are outside the idea.

Compression has a cost. Leave out too much, and the result is a postcard. Take in too much, and it stops being the small fiction he means. These notes name that tension and leave it unresolved. The [references](references.md) include two other efforts that sit in a similar tension. They are not a specification, and they are not this idea's competitors.

## A later proof, not a start

Experience-first remains the aspiration. Moving through the place should feel like lived Chennai before it feels like a list of systems. That aspiration is about quality. It is not a rule that a future public experiment must wait until the feeling is finished, and it is not permission to start one now.

The future aspiration, after career commitments, is a small proof of concept that other people could join publicly. The wish is that this would come sooner rather than later once those commitments allow. Sooner rather than later is not a date. The proof is not authorized. What "small" includes is undecided. These notes are not that proof.

Server and hosting expense is a future question of whether such a proof is feasible. These notes do no research on it and give no estimate.

## An existing game with the same name

Other people have a Chennai game under the name Project Madras. Venkat watched their showcase, [Project Madras - OpenWorld Gameplay Showcase](https://youtu.be/ra-lYQAhbB8?si=ClY5YFNWclMkVFTL). He describes years of hard work, and he loves their project. He describes the game as an existing independent commercial project. Care for the city and commercial work can belong to the same people. These notes do not suggest otherwise.

His idea differs in kind. It is a fictionalized city inspired by Chennai, on a much smaller geography, using an 80/20 cut toward core highlights, and aiming at density of places, activities, and micro-interactions, with a lived feel and a realistic visual ambition. It is not a competing full-city recreation, and it is not an imitation of their game.

These notes do not take scope, cost, or history from that video. The reference entry says what was and was not established.

## Opening tone

Venkat has suggested an intentional funny spawn for an early version, so a rough first play would set lighter expectations. The humor in that suggestion is nonpolitical, and it is about the unfinished build. It is not a joke at the city's expense. It is optional. It is not a decided requirement.

The suggestion sits beside his account of Hyd World in the [references](references.md): a rough but recognizable city, and an unscripted moment that was funny because nobody had written it. If anything from that account is worth remembering here, it is lightness about a rough build. That account's players, combat, and names stay in the account.

If an early version ever used that kind of opening, the further suggestion is to retire it in favor of a mature, polished opening. The thought is about tone. It has not been adopted, and it schedules nothing.

A separate idea was only tentative: a politically themed opening built around a chief-minister convoy associated with Vijay. Venkat flagged the risk of offense. It is not an approved feature. Political figures, parties, and convoys are outside this project.
